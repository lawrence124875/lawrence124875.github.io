A spread is the set of rules for where cards go and what each position means. Beginners often jump straight into ten-card spreads, get overwhelmed and give up. Starting with one to three cards builds core reading skills much faster.

## Why start small?

- **The right amount of information:** focus on just one to three cards.
- **Practise connections:** three cards are enough to learn how cards relate.
- **Easy to review:** simple to record and compare later.

## One card: the daily draw

**Layout:** shuffle and draw one card.

**Good for:** a daily reminder or a quick check on how you are right now.

**How to read it:** keep it simple. Ask yourself, "What about today does this image remind me of?" A single card is the best way to practise **observation and association**.

## Two-option spread

**Layout:** one card (or two) on the left and one on the right, for option A and option B.

| Position | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Left | How option A looks, or where it may lead |
| Right | How option B looks, or where it may lead |

**Good for:** when you're torn between two choices.

**Remember:** the spread doesn't make the decision for you. It helps you see sides of each option you hadn't noticed.

## Three-card spread: the most versatile starter

Lay three cards left to right. You can adapt what the positions mean to your question:

| Version | Card 1 | Card 2 | Card 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timeline | Past | Present | Possible future |
| Action | Situation | What you can do | Likely outcome |
| Relationship | You | The other person | The relationship |
| Mind, body, spirit | Body | Mood | Inner self |

**How to read it:** look at each card on its own first, then see whether the three tell a story together. If the first and third cards feel very different, the middle card is often the key to the change.

## Tips for practice

1. **Decide the spread and positions before you draw.** Don't change them afterwards.
2. **Start with one card** for a week or two, then move on to three.
3. **Write every reading down:** question, spread, cards and your interpretation.
4. **Once three cards feel easy**, try a five-card spread or the Celtic Cross.

More cards don't make a better spread. What matters is reading something meaningful from each position. Three cards read well beat ten cards read in a muddle.
