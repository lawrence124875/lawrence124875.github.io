The most common worry for new tarot readers is: "How am I supposed to remember 78 cards?" The good news is that tarot is built on clear patterns. With the right approach, you never need to memorise card by card.

## Method 1: Learn the structure before the details

The 78 cards fall into clear groups:

- **22 Major Arcana**: numbered 0 to 21, showing life's big themes
- **56 Minor Arcana**: four suits (Wands, Cups, Swords and Pentacles) with 14 cards each
  - Number cards: Ace to 10
  - Court cards: Page, Knight, Queen and King

Remember that **the suit tells you the area of life and the number tells you the stage**. Then the Minor Arcana become a 4 × 14 grid instead of 56 separate things.

## Method 2: Tell the Major Arcana as a story

The Major Arcana are often seen as a journey. It starts with card 0, The Fool, meets different figures and challenges along the way, and ends at card 21. Try telling the 22 cards in order as a story in your own words. A story is far easier to remember than a list, and it shows where each card fits in the whole.

## Method 3: Look before you read

Pick up a card and spend a minute observing before you look anything up:

- What are the people doing? What are their expressions?
- What are the main colours, and how do they feel?
- Which objects catch your eye?

Write down what you notice, then compare it with a guidebook. **What you discover yourself sticks longer than what you read.**

## Method 4: Draw one card a day

Draw a card each morning, think about how it might relate to your day, and review it in the evening. Over a year, each card turns up four or five times on average, each time linked to real experience, so the memories go deeper.

## Method 5: Keep a tarot journal

Give each card its own page and note:

1. Your first impression
2. Key symbols in the image
3. What was happening in your life when it appeared in a reading

Over time, the journal becomes **a guidebook written just for you**, more useful than any ready-made book.

## A practical schedule

| Weeks | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Major Arcana, 2–3 cards a day, linked as a story |
| 3–4 | The four suits and what the numbers mean |
| 5–6 | Court cards, pictured as people you know |
| After that | A daily card and your journal |

Learning tarot isn't an exam, and you don't need to learn it all at once. The goal is for each card to slowly become a familiar friend.
