The Celtic Cross may be the best-known tarot spread. It became popular in the early twentieth century through A. E. Waite's writing. Its 10 cards look at a situation from every side: the present, obstacles, past, future, and inner and outer influences.

## The layout

The spread has two parts: the "cross" on the left and the "staff" on the right.

```
          5
   4    1/2    6        10
          3              9
                         8
                         7
```

- **1** goes in the centre, with **2** laid sideways across it.
- **3** goes below, **4** left, **5** above and **6** right.
- **7 to 10** form a column on the right, from bottom to top.

## What the 10 positions mean

| Position | Name | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Present | The heart of the matter, where you are now |
| 2 | Challenge | The obstacle crossing you, or an influence to deal with |
| 3 | Foundation | The root of the situation, unconscious factors |
| 4 | Recent past | Influences that are fading |
| 5 | Goal | What you consciously hope for, the best possibility |
| 6 | Near future | What may happen soon |
| 7 | Self | Your attitude and how you see the situation |
| 8 | Environment | People around you and outside conditions |
| 9 | Hopes and fears | What you hope for or fear (often the same thing) |
| 10 | Outcome | Where things may lead if they continue as they are |

Readers differ slightly on positions 3, 4, 5 and 6. Choose one version and stick with it.

## Four steps for beginners

### 1. Look at the whole picture

After turning all 10 cards, don't rush card by card. Notice: are there many Major Arcana? Does one suit dominate? Is the overall feel bright or heavy? This gives you a general direction.

### 2. Read the central cross

Positions 1 and 2 are the core of the spread. First work out what's happening now and where you're stuck.

### 3. Follow the timeline

Link 3 and 4 (roots and past) with 5 and 6 (goal and near future) to see where things came from and where they're heading.

### 4. Read the staff

Positions 7 to 10 are about people: you, those around you, your feelings, and finally the outcome. Pay special attention to how 10 relates to 1 and 5. Does the outcome match what you hope for? If not, 7 and 8 often show what you can adjust.

## Common questions

**Can beginners start with the Celtic Cross?** Yes, but get comfortable with three-card spreads first. Ten cards is a lot of information, and it's easier with a foundation.

**What if the outcome card looks bad?** The outcome shows where things may go on the current path. It isn't fixed. Look back at your attitude in position 7. It often shows what you can change.

**What if I can't read it all at once?** Take a photo and read it over a few days. Many people only understand a spread when they look back later.
