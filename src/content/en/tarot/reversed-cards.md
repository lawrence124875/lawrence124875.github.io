When you shuffle, some cards end up upside down. These are "reversed" cards. Whether to read them is one of the most common beginner questions. The answer: **there's no single rule, but decide on yours before you start.**

## Why do reversals exist?

Nothing in tarot itself says you must read reversals. Reading them developed later among readers who wanted more layers from each card. That's why approaches vary so much.

## Approach 1: Ignore reversals

Turn all cards upright when shuffling, or simply turn reversed cards upright when you draw them.

**Pros:** beginners only need to learn 78 meanings, which keeps things manageable.
**Cons:** you lose a way to show "stuck" or "not flowing", so you rely on positions and surrounding cards instead.

**Good for:** people who are just starting and still learning the cards.

## Approach 2: Reversals as blocked or delayed energy

Read a reversed card as its energy **not fully expressed yet**, held back or delayed, rather than the complete opposite.

**Pros:** the card keeps its theme and you only adjust its strength or timing, so readings feel natural.
**Cons:** working out where the block is takes some experience.

**Good for:** people who know the upright cards and want more nuance.

## Approach 3: Reversals as inner energy

Read a reversed card as its theme happening **inside you** rather than in outside events. For example, a card about action could mean you want to act but haven't yet.

**Pros:** especially good for self-reflection questions.
**Cons:** harder to apply to concrete events.

**Good for:** people who use tarot for journaling and inner work.

## The three approaches compared

| | Ignore | Blocked energy | Inner energy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Difficulty | Low | Medium | Medium |
| Depth | Simpler | More nuanced | Inward-looking |
| Best for | Any question | How events develop | Self-exploration |

## Advice for beginners

1. **Skip reversals at first** and focus on getting to know each card's image and theme.
2. **After a month or two**, if your readings feel too flat, add reversals.
3. **Pick one approach and stick with it.** Don't change the rules when you draw a card you don't like.
4. **Decide before you shuffle** whether this reading uses reversals.

A reversed card isn't a "bad card". It invites you to look at the same theme from another angle. Used well, it brings your readings closer to real life.
