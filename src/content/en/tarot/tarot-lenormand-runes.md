If you're new to divination tools, you may be torn between tarot, Lenormand and runes. All three can help you sort out your thoughts and notice angles you missed, but they're built and read very differently. This guide compares them simply so you can decide where to start.

## The differences at a glance

| | Tarot | Lenormand | Runes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Size | 78 cards | 36 cards | 24 runes (some sets add one blank rune) |
| Origin | A 15th-century Italian card game, used for divination from the 18th century | A 19th-century German card game, named after the French fortune teller Mlle Lenormand | An old Germanic alphabet, originally a writing system |
| Imagery | Full scenes with people | One simple, everyday object | A letter made of straight lines |
| Reading focus | Symbols and story of each card | How two or more cards combine, and where they sit | The core idea of a single symbol |
| Best for | Feelings, relationships, personal growth | Concrete events and near-term developments | Short reminders and today's lesson |

## Tarot: telling stories with pictures

Tarot has 22 Major Arcana and 56 Minor Arcana cards. The Major Arcana show the big stages of life, while the Minor Arcana use four suits to cover different areas of daily life.

Tarot's strength is its **rich imagery**. Every card has figures, actions and backgrounds, so details like colour, expression and direction can spark many ideas. That's also what makes it hard: 78 cards, each with layers of symbolism, can feel like a lot for beginners.

Tarot suits you if you:

- want to explore feelings, motives and relationships
- like making up stories from images
- are happy to build skill slowly

## Lenormand: reading cards like sentences

Lenormand has only 36 cards, each showing one simple thing such as a house, a ship or a bouquet. Reading it is more like **building a sentence**: a single card's meaning is usually direct, and the real information comes from how two or three cards modify each other, like an adjective and a noun.

With fewer cards and simpler pictures, Lenormand is quicker to pick up than tarot. Reading it well takes practice with combinations, and the classic large spread lays out all 36 cards at once.

Lenormand suits you if you:

- ask concrete, practical questions
- like clear rules and puzzle-like thinking
- want to get started quickly

## Runes: one symbol, one reminder

Runes come from the ancient alphabets of northern Europe. The most common set is the Elder Futhark, with 24 letters. Today they're usually carved on stones or wood and drawn from a bag.

Runes have no pictures. Each symbol stands for one **core idea**, so readings are short, often just one to three runes. They won't unfold a long story like tarot, but they work well as a daily reminder or when you need to clear your head quickly.

Runes suit you if you:

- like simple, abstract symbols
- want a one-minute daily practice
- are curious about Norse history and culture

## Which should a beginner learn first?

There's no single right answer. Choose by the kind of question you want to ask:

1. **To understand yourself** → start with tarot, beginning with the 22 Major Arcana
2. **To see how a situation may develop** → start with Lenormand, practising two-card combinations
3. **For a simple daily habit** → start with runes, drawing one each day

The three don't compete. Many people build a foundation with one, then try the others and find that the ways of thinking complement each other.

## Tips for getting started

- **Ask one question at a time.** The clearer the question, the more useful the reading.
- **Write down your results.** Look back a few days later and you'll learn about your own thinking habits.
- **Treat it as a tool for reflection**, not as an answer that decides for you.
