To use the I Ching (Book of Changes) to think through a question, you first need to **cast a hexagram**: use a method with an element of chance to get six lines, which together form one of the 64 hexagrams. There are many methods. These are the three beginners meet most often.

## Prepare your question first

Whatever method you use, get clear on your question before you start. Ask about one thing at a time. Questions like "How should I approach…?" or "What happens if I choose…?" usually give more useful readings than "Will it happen?" (See also: [How to ask the I Ching a question](/en/iching/how-to-ask-a-question/).)

## Method 1: The three-coin method

This is the most popular method today. All you need is three identical coins.

1. Hold your question in mind and toss all three coins together.
2. From the mix of heads and tails, note whether the line is yin or yang, and whether it is a changing line.
3. Repeat six times, drawing the lines from the **bottom up**.

**Pros:** you always have the tools, and a reading takes two or three minutes.
**Cons:** you need to remember the scoring rule, which is easy to get wrong at first.

For full steps, see [How to cast the I Ching with three coins](/en/iching/coin-method/).

## Method 2: Plum Blossom numerology

Plum Blossom numerology (Meihua Yishu) is traditionally credited to the Song-dynasty scholar Shao Yong. Its big advantage: **no tools at all**. The usual approach takes the current year, month, day and hour, or any numbers that come to mind, and uses simple division to find the upper trigram, the lower trigram and the changing line.

**Pros:** you can cast anywhere, and it builds familiarity with the eight trigrams.
**Cons:** you need to learn the trigram numbers first, so it's a little harder than coins.

## Method 3: Yarrow stalks

This is the ancient method described in the Great Commentary (Xici). You divide and count fifty yarrow stalks (or sticks) several times to produce a single line. A full hexagram often takes 15 to 30 minutes.

**Pros:** the slow process works like a meditation, and many people find it more focused.
**Cons:** the steps are complex, you need special tools, and the procedure is hard for beginners to remember.

## The three methods compared

| | Three coins | Plum Blossom | Yarrow stalks |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tools | Three coins | None | Fifty stalks |
| Time | About 3 minutes | About 1 minute | 15–30 minutes |
| Difficulty | Low | Medium | High |
| Best for | Your first readings | Practising anytime | A sense of ritual |

## Our advice for beginners

Start with the coins. They teach you two core ideas: lines are built **from the bottom up**, and **some lines change**. Once you can see how the primary hexagram relates to the changed one, Plum Blossom numerology will come much more easily.

Casting is only the start. The real value comes from what you think afterwards: what does this hexagram make you notice, and what will you do about it? Writing that down matters more than the answer itself.
