The three-coin method needs nothing but three coins, and it's the way most people cast the I Ching today. This guide walks you through your first hexagram.

## What you need

- **Three identical coins**, so heads and tails are easy to tell apart
- **Pen and paper** to record each toss
- **One clear question**: ask about one thing at a time

## Step 1: Give heads and tails a value

The most common scoring is:

- Heads (decide in advance which side counts) = **3**
- Tails = **2**

Added together, three coins can only give 6, 7, 8 or 9:

| Total | Coins | Name | Line | Changing? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | Three tails | Old yin | Broken ⚋ | Yes, becomes yang |
| 7 | One heads, two tails | Young yang | Solid ⚊ | No |
| 8 | Two heads, one tails | Young yin | Broken ⚋ | No |
| 9 | Three heads | Old yang | Solid ⚊ | Yes, becomes yin |

An easy way to remember it: **odd is yang, even is yin; 6 and 9 are "old", and old lines change.**

## Step 2: Toss six times, recording from the bottom up

1. Hold the coins in your hands and think of your question.
2. Toss, add up the total, and record the line.
3. Repeat six times.

Your first toss is the **bottom line**, and your sixth is the **top line**. This is the most common beginner mistake, so try writing from the bottom of the page upwards.

## Step 3: Draw the primary and changed hexagrams

The six lines make your **primary hexagram**. If any lines are 6 or 9 (changing lines), flip them to their opposite. The new hexagram is the **changed hexagram** (also called the relating hexagram). With no changing lines, you only read the primary one.

## A worked example

| Toss | Total | Line | Position |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6th | 8 | Yin | Top |
| 5th | 7 | Yang | Fifth |
| 4th | 9 | Yang (changing) | Fourth |
| 3rd | 8 | Yin | Third |
| 2nd | 7 | Yang | Second |
| 1st | 6 | Yin (changing) | Bottom |

This reading has two changing lines (bottom and fourth), so there's a changed hexagram too. Next, look up both hexagrams and think about how they relate to your question.

## Common questions

**Can I use different coins?** Yes, as long as you decide in advance which side is heads.

**Can I keep re-casting the same question?** It's best not to. The first cast best reflects your thinking in the moment. Re-casting tends to become a search for the answer you want.

**Can I swap the values for heads and tails?** Some traditions do. Choosing one rule and sticking with it matters more than which one is "right".
