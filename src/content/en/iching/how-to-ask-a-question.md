Many people cast a hexagram and feel they "can't read it". Often the problem isn't the hexagram but a vague question. The clearer your question, the more useful the reading. Here are five practical rules.

## Rule 1: Ask about one thing at a time

"Should I change jobs, and will my relationship go well?" is two questions. When you read the hexagram, you won't know which part answers which. Split it and cast twice.

## Rule 2: Ask "how", not "will it"

"Will I succeed?" invites a yes-or-no, and you'll end up looking only for good or bad signs. Ask "What attitude should I bring to this opportunity?" and the reading gives you something you can act on.

## Rule 3: Put yourself in the question

The I Ching is about situations and how to respond to them. Instead of "What is he thinking?", ask "What can I do in this relationship?" Focusing on what you can control makes the reading easier to apply.

## Rule 4: Add a time frame

"What should I pay attention to in my new team over the next three months?" is far more specific than "How will my career go?" A time frame also lets you compare when you look back.

## Rule 5: Ask about what you truly care about

Asking just to test it, or asking the same thing again and again, usually gives muddled results. Wait until you have something you genuinely want to understand, then cast with a calm mind.

## Vague questions vs better questions

| Vague | Better |
|---|---|
| Will I pass the exam? | What do I most need to change while I prepare? |
| Does she like me? | How can I express myself in this relationship? |
| What will this year bring? | Where should I focus my energy at work this year? |
| Should I move, and which flat should I pick? | If I move to A, what should I watch out for? |

## Simple habits before and after casting

- **Before:** write the question down, read it back, and check it's about one thing.
- **While casting:** take a few quiet seconds and focus on the question.
- **After:** write down your first thought before you study the structure.
- **Later:** revisit your notes and compare your thoughts with what actually happened.

The value of consulting the I Ching is that it makes you stop and think clearly. A good question is often half the answer.
