"I learned it yesterday, and today it's gone." If that sounds familiar, the problem probably isn't your memory but your method. Here are the four most common mistakes.

## Mistake 1: Learning without reviewing

**Why you forget:** new information fades fastest in the first day or two. Learn a word once and never look again, and most of it disappears quickly.

**How to fix it:**

- Review the next day, three days later and a week later.
- Split each study session into "review old words" and "learn new words", and review first.

## Mistake 2: Memorising only "English = translation"

**Why you forget:** one pairing gives your memory too few cues. Many words also have several meanings, so if you only know one translation, you won't recognise the word in other contexts.

**How to fix it:**

- Learn each word with **an example sentence**.
- Listen to the **pronunciation** so sound becomes a cue too.
- Notice common **collocations**, such as *make a decision* or *take a break*.

## Mistake 3: Learning too many at once

**Why you forget:** cram 100 new words into a day and your brain can't absorb them. The next day's review pile also explodes, and you soon give up.

**How to fix it:**

- Stick to 10–30 new words a day.
- A little every day beats bursts followed by breaks.
- Learn in frequency order. The most common words give you the most value per word.

## Mistake 4: Reading without recalling

**Why you forget:** rereading a word list creates a feeling of "I think I know this", but when you need the word, it won't come.

**How to fix it:**

- When you see or hear the English, **cover the answer** and try to say the meaning.
- Practise the other way too: see the meaning and try to say the English.
- It's fine if you can't remember. The effort of trying to recall strengthens the memory.

## A better daily routine

1. **Review:** go over recent words using active recall.
2. **Learn new words:** 10–30 of them, each with pronunciation and an example sentence.
3. **Listen again in spare moments:** replay today's words while commuting or doing chores.
4. **Recall before bed:** try to remember three to five of today's words.

## Summary

Forgetting words is normal. Even native speakers forget words they rarely use. The goal isn't to never forget. It's to use methods that let common words come back to you when you need them.
