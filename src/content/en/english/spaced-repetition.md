Learning words and forgetting them a few days later happens to everyone. Spaced repetition tackles exactly this problem: **review just before you forget, and remember for longest with the fewest reviews.**

## It starts with the forgetting curve

In the late nineteenth century, German psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus ran a series of memory experiments and found that:

- New information is forgotten fastest in the first few hours to a day
- After that, forgetting slows down
- Each review makes the next round of forgetting slower still

This "fast, then slow" curve is the famous **forgetting curve**.

## How spaced repetition works

Since each review makes a memory a little more stable, the gaps between reviews can **grow longer each time**:

- Right after learning, a memory is fragile, so review soon.
- After a few reviews, the memory is steadier, so you can wait longer.
- Reviewing when you're "about to forget, but haven't yet" works best.

Compared with repeating something many times in one sitting, spreading the same number of reviews over different days usually makes it last longer.

## A simple review schedule

Here is one easy-to-remember schedule. Adjust it to suit you:

| Review | Time since last study |
|---|---|
| 1st | Next day |
| 2nd | 3 days later |
| 3rd | 1 week later |
| 4th | 2 weeks later |
| 5th | 1 month later |

If you find you've forgotten a word, move it back to a shorter interval.

## No app needed: the Leitner box

German journalist Sebastian Leitner designed a paper-based method:

1. Prepare 5 boxes (or envelopes). All word cards start in box 1.
2. Review box 1 daily, box 2 every 2–3 days, box 3 weekly, and so on.
3. Move cards you get right to the next box. Cards you get wrong go back to box 1.

This naturally means you see familiar words less and difficult words more.

## Spaced repetition by listening

You don't have to use cards. If you prefer learning by ear:

- Split words into small sets, such as 50 words each.
- Listen to a new set for several days in a row.
- Then return to older sets following the schedule above.

## Tips to make it work better

- **Active recall:** when you see or hear the English, try to think of the meaning before checking.
- **Don't overload:** 10–30 new words a day is easier to sustain.
- **Use example sentences:** words learned in sentences stick better than words alone.
- **Pick a fixed time**, such as your commute or before bed.

The key to spaced repetition isn't studying harder. It's meeting each word again at the right time.
