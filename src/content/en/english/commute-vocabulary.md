Thirty minutes of commuting a day, five days a week, adds up to more than 120 hours a year. Use even part of that for vocabulary and the results add up remarkably.

## Why spare moments suit vocabulary

- **Words are small units.** You can meet a word in a few seconds, with no need for long sessions.
- **Spaced practice beats cramming.** The same total time split into short sessions usually sticks better than one long session.
- **Habits form easily.** Tying learning to your daily commute is easier to keep up than "I'll study when I have time".

## Method 1: Listen

Your ears are your best tool on the move. Prepare audio that plays "English word → meaning → example sentence", put on your headphones and go. You don't need to look at a screen while walking or changing trains. (See also: [Can you learn vocabulary by listening?](/en/english/learn-words-by-listening/))

## Method 2: Recall during the pause

When you hear the English word, try to say the meaning before you hear it. This "guess, then check" step makes words stick far better than listening alone.

## Method 3: One small goal per trip

For example: "finish 30 words this trip" or "replay yesterday's 20 words before I get off". Small goals are easy to reach and feel rewarding.

## Method 4: Review first, then learn new words

Use the first half of the trip, when you're fresher, to review yesterday's words, and the second half for new ones. Reviewing first rescues words you're about to forget and warms up your brain.

## Method 5: One minute of recall after you arrive

On the walk to work or school, try to recall three words you just heard. That minute of recall is often worth more than ten extra minutes of listening.

## Tips for different kinds of travel

| Travel | Suggested approach |
|---|---|
| Train or bus (seated) | Listen, and glance at spellings now and then |
| Train or bus (standing) | Listen only, with your screen locked |
| Walking | Listen only, at low volume, and stay aware of your surroundings |
| Driving or cycling | Safety first: listen only at low volume when it's safe, or review after you arrive |

## How to keep it going

- **Prepare the night before.** Choose what to listen to so you can just press play.
- **Play with the screen locked.** You won't keep checking your phone, and it saves battery.
- **Don't aim to understand everything at once.** Repeated exposure matters more than learning in one go.
- **Track your streak.** Tick each day on a calendar. A growing streak is motivating.

Your commute will pass either way. The only difference is what you do with it. A little every day, and a year from now you'll be glad you started.
