Most people learn vocabulary with their eyes, but we first learn language with our ears. Learning words by listening isn't just "something to do on the commute". There's real memory science behind it.

## Why listening helps

### 1. You learn sound and meaning together

Learning only by reading often leaves you able to read a word but not catch it in speech. Listening **ties pronunciation to meaning from the start**, so you react faster when you hear the word in conversations or videos.

### 2. Your brain has a memory loop for sound

Psychologist Alan Baddeley's working memory model includes a part that briefly holds sounds, called the "phonological loop". Research links it closely to learning new words. Hearing and repeating words helps move them into long-term memory.

### 3. Dual coding

Hearing the English word and its meaning in your own language gives you two cues for the same word. The more cues you have, the better your chances of recalling it later.

### 4. You can use spare moments

When you're walking, commuting or doing chores, your eyes and hands are busy but your ears are free. Listening turns that time into learning time.

## The limits of learning by listening

- **Spelling needs separate practice.** If you only listen, spelling can stay fuzzy.
- **Attention drifts.** If audio plays in the background and you're not really listening, the effect drops.
- **Material matters.** Clear pronunciation, a comfortable pace and pauses that give you time to react make a big difference.

So listening works best as **one of your main ways of meeting words**, combined with a little reading and writing.

## A practical routine

1. **Choose the right order.** Start with the most frequent words, such as a frequency-ranked core list.
2. **Listen in an "English → meaning → example sentence" rhythm.** Hear the word, then its meaning, then hear it once more in a sentence.
3. **Say it out loud.** Repeat quietly or in your head. It sticks better than just listening.
4. **Guess during the pause.** After the English word, try to say the meaning before you hear it. This "active recall" is very effective.
5. **Listen in small sets**, such as 20 to 50 words, repeat them for a few days, then move on.
6. **Check spelling now and then.** A few minutes a week reading the words fills the gap that listening leaves.

## Summary

Learning words by listening has solid reasons behind it, and some limits. Make it a daily habit, meet core words again and again in spare moments, and add a little reading and spelling practice. It's one of the least effortful ways to grow your vocabulary.
